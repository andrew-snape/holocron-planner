## Designing for macOS

People rely on the power, spaciousness and flexibility of a Mac for in-depth productivity tasks, viewing media and content, and playing games, often with several apps open at once.

### Device characteristics

- **Display:** a Mac usually has a large, high-resolution display, and people can extend their workspace with more displays, including an iPad.
- **Ergonomics:** people usually use a Mac while stationary, often on a desk, with the screen about 1 to 3 feet away.
- **Inputs:** people expect to combine keyboards, pointing devices, game controllers and Siri.
- **App interactions:** sessions can last a few minutes or several hours of concentration. People often have several apps open and expect smooth transitions between active and inactive states.
- **System features:** the menu bar, file management, full screen, and Dock menus all work in familiar, consistent ways.

### Best practices

- **Use large displays well.** Present more content in fewer nested levels, with less modality. Keep information density comfortable, so people do not strain to see what they need.
- **Let people resize, hide, show and move windows** to suit how they work. Support full screen for a distraction-free context.
- **Put every command in the menu bar.** It gives people easy access to everything they need in your app.
- **Support precise input.** People expect pixel-level selection and editing with a pointer or trackpad.
- **Handle keyboard shortcuts** to speed up actions and support keyboard-only work.
- **Support personalisation.** Let people customise toolbars, set up windows to show the views they use most, and choose colours and fonts.

## Designing for iPadOS

People value the power, mobility and flexibility of an iPad as they enjoy media, play games, do detailed productivity work and make things.

### Device characteristics

- **Display:** iPad has a large, high-resolution display.
- **Ergonomics:** people often hold an iPad, but they also set it on a surface or a stand. Viewing distance changes with the position, but people are usually within about 3 feet of it.
- **Inputs:** people use multi-touch gestures, virtual keyboards, an attached keyboard or pointing device, Apple Pencil and voice, often in combination.
- **App interactions:** a session can be a few quick actions or hours of immersion. People often have several apps open and like to see more than one at once, using capabilities such as drag and drop between apps.
- **System features:** multitasking, widgets and drag and drop work in familiar ways across apps.

### Best practices

- **Use the large display to bring the content forward.** Minimise modal interfaces and full-screen transitions. Place controls where they are easy to reach, but not in the way.
- **Use viewing distance and input mode** to decide the size and density of on-screen content.
- **Support several inputs.** Let people use multi-touch gestures, a keyboard or trackpad, or Apple Pencil, and consider interactions that combine them.
- **Adapt to change.** Handle device rotation, multitasking modes, Dark Mode and Dynamic Type. Let people run the app in the configuration that suits them, including on the Mac where that makes sense.

## Notes for this project

For Holocron, the macOS guidance supports a windowed planner with a menu bar carrying every command, full keyboard shortcuts, and a layout that works in split screen. The iPadOS guidance matters only if you later open the planner on an iPad, where the menu bar is hidden until revealed and the layout must adapt to narrower widths.
